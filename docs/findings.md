# Ledger Odyssey: what one year of a Tangerine style ledger actually says

> **SYNTHETIC DATA NOTICE: every number below comes from a synthetic mock ledger.** Source: `data/mock/tangerine_transactions.csv`, generated by `scripts/generate_mock_ledger.py` with seed `20261107` (5,702 rows, 2025-11-01 to 2026-10-31, Tangerine style schema). No real bank statements are used anywhere in this repository. Every method here transfers unchanged to the real Tangerine dataset: only the input CSV path changes, because scripts/build_world.py already consumes either file.

## The question we asked

Of one year of Tangerine style transactions, how much spending is locked by the calendar rather than by choice, when does the balance actually run out, and what would it cost to move spending onto the payday cycle? The 2026 rubric weights Discovery at 35 and Data Use at 25, so this entry leads with the findings; the orbit game below them is the exploration layer over the same numbers.

## What we did

| Step | What ran | Artifact |
|------|----------|----------|
| 1. Build | `scripts/build_world.py` turns the CSV into a row table plus a game world, stamping per entity provenance (row ids, filter, aggregation, SHA256 checksum) | `public/data/world.json`, `public/data/transactions.json` |
| 2. F1 structural share | Day of week and holiday deseasonalized lag autocorrelation at lags 28 to 35 per category, thresholded against a white noise band and control lags 50 to 180 | `findings.json` f1 |
| 3. F2 seasonality | Day of week, month, and holiday window decomposition with declared verdict thresholds | `findings.json` f2 |
| 4. F3 risk model | L2 logistic regression on night before features (trailing windows end at t minus 1), time ordered split at 2026-02-01, C selected by time ordered CV inside the train window, model card emitted | `public/data/model_card.json` |
| 5. F4 plane change cost | Counterfactual timing simulation of every category against the income phase, three scenarios each | `findings.json` f4 |
| 6. F5 interventions | Four simulated counterfactuals ranked by floor lift then drawdown improvement | `findings.json` f5 |
| 7. Verify | `scripts/analysis/verify_findings.py` recomputes every number and compares against the files, mirroring the pipeline checksum discipline; `uv run pytest` wraps it | exit code 0 |

Seeds and versions: mock ledger seed `20261107`, scikit-learn `random_state 20261107`, dependencies pinned in `pyproject.toml` with `uv.lock`.

## What we found

### F1: only 5.08% of annual spend is structural (calendar locked); 94.92% is discretionary

Question: Which dollars are locked by the calendar and which are choices?

We built one daily spend series per category over 2025-11-01 to 2026-10-31, removed the day of week mean effect and the holiday window mean effect (so weekly rhythm and the one holiday level shift cannot masquerade as monthly recurrence), then tested lag autocorrelation at lags 28 to 35 against both a white noise band and the category's own control lags. **Subscriptions, Utilities are structural; Dining, Groceries, Retail, Transit, Travel are discretionary.**

| Category | Annual spend | Peak acf (lag 28 to 35) | Threshold | Classification |
|----------|-------------|--------------------------|-----------|----------------|
| Dining | $27,766.09 | 0.075 at lag 30 | 0.103 | discretionary |
| Groceries | $16,458.88 | 0.054 at lag 31 | 0.103 | discretionary |
| Retail | $19,926.95 | 0.058 at lag 30 | 0.103 | discretionary |
| Subscriptions | $976.50 | 0.226 at lag 28 | 0.141 | structural |
| Transit | $11,126.07 | 0.020 at lag 34 | 0.103 | discretionary |
| Travel | $4,448.56 | 0.048 at lag 35 | 0.103 | discretionary |
| Utilities | $3,291.88 | 0.330 at lag 30 | 0.132 | structural |

So what: the persona's problem is behavioral, not structural. The dollar total that survives the recurrence test is only $4,268.38 out of $83,994.93. The lag window tests monthly recurrence only. Weekly locked behavior (for example weekday commuting in Transit) counts as discretionary here by construction; F2 measures that weekly pattern separately.

### F2: seasonality is mixed (calendar driven and behavioral, split by category)

Question: When in the week and the year does this person actually spend?

Every category's spend is split into 7 day of week shares, 12 ledger month shares, and the 47 day holiday window share, each compared against the share of days it covers. Verdict rules: holiday lift at or above 1.25 is calendar driven; weekly deviation (weekend share versus its 2/7 calendar baseline) at or above 0.2 is behavioral and needs at least 30 debit rows so sparse weekday noise cannot qualify; both is mixed; neither is weak.

| Category | Weekend share vs calendar | Holiday lift | Weekly deviation | Verdict |
|----------|---------------------------|--------------|------------------|---------|
| Dining | 35.4% vs 28.6% (1.24x) | 1.47x | 24% | mixed (calendar driven holiday plus behavioral weekly rhythm) |
| Groceries | 34.5% vs 28.6% (1.21x) | 1.08x | 21% | behavioral |
| Retail | 28.0% vs 28.6% (0.98x) | 1.80x | 2% | structural (calendar driven) |
| Subscriptions | 30.8% vs 28.6% (1.08x) | 0.97x | 8% | weak or no seasonality |
| Transit | 18.3% vs 28.6% (0.64x) | 1.52x | 36% | mixed (calendar driven holiday plus behavioral weekly rhythm) |
| Travel | 0.0% vs 28.6% (0.00x) | 2.94x | 100% | structural (calendar driven) |
| Utilities | 29.6% vs 28.6% (1.03x) | 0.63x | 3% | weak or no seasonality |

Plain language: Retail is the most calendar driven (1.80 times its holiday calendar share) and Transit is the most behavioral (weekend deviation 36% away from the 2/7 calendar baseline). behavioral: Groceries; mixed (calendar driven holiday plus behavioral weekly rhythm): Dining, Transit; structural (calendar driven): Retail, Travel; weak or no seasonality: Subscriptions, Utilities.

Note: Travel has fewer than 30 debit rows, so its weekday columns are shown for completeness and only the calendar test applies to its verdict.

### F3: the deficit day model reaches test AUC 0.590 (accuracy 62.6%, majority baseline 86.1%)

Question: Can the ledger predict a deficit day before it happens?

Target: a deficit day is a day whose closing balance is negative (0 CAD threshold, 86 events). The data supports this label better than the next day below 1,000 CAD alternative, which we also fit as a robustness check (test AUC 0.585). Features (every row is known the night before day t: trailing windows end at t minus 1, so day t never enters its own feature row): trailing 7 and 30 day net flow, day of week and month indicators, days since payroll, trailing 30 day spend volatility. Time ordered split at 2026-02-01 (91 train days with 48 events, 273 test days with 38 events); C = 1.0 chosen by time ordered CV on train only.

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Test AUC (headline) | 0.590 |
| Train AUC (drift check) | 0.957 |
| Accuracy at 0.5 threshold | 62.6% |
| Majority class baseline accuracy | 86.1% |
| Confusion (tn, fp, fn, tp) | 158, 77, 25, 13 |

Top coefficients (standardized, with direction):

| Feature | Coefficient | Direction |
|---------|-------------|-----------|
| spend_volatility_30d | +1.6223 | a one standard deviation increase raises the odds of a deficit day |
| days_since_payday | +1.5547 | a one standard deviation increase raises the odds of a deficit day |
| m_1 | +0.7744 | presence of this indicator raises the odds versus the reference level |
| dow_2 | +0.6139 | presence of this indicator raises the odds versus the reference level |
| net_flow_7d | -0.5495 | a one standard deviation increase lowers the odds of a deficit day |

So what: the strongest signal is spend_volatility_30d (+1.6223: a one standard deviation increase raises the odds of a deficit day), followed by days_since_payday (+1.5547). The model card in `public/data/model_card.json` carries the intended use, the drift limitation, the fairness caveats, and the correlation not causation statement.

### F4: the plane change cost, aligning Travel to the income phase buys $1,005.93 of drawdown (4.6 percent of the $21,884.01 baseline drawdown) and lifts the true trough by $1,005.93

Question: What does it cost to move a category's spending onto the income phase (the plane change cost)?

Definition (exact): For a given category, every debit with pay cycle phase p (days since the most recent payroll deposit, with a virtual deposit anchored at 2025-10-25) of 7 or more is moved forward to (next payroll date) + (p mod 7) days, landing it in the first week after the following deposit; debits with phase 0 to 6 are already in the income window and do not move. The daily balance path is rebuilt from the same opening balance with identical amounts, and peak, trough, drawdown, and month end closes are recomputed over the 365 day ledger. The reverse direction is computed two ways: the observed ledger (left misaligned, delta zero by construction, benefit forgone equals the aligned delta) and an anti aligned stress case that moves every debit of the category to the day before the next deposit.

| Category | Aligned drawdown improvement | Aligned trough lift | Aligned floor delta | Anti aligned floor delta |
|----------|------------------------------|---------------------|---------------------|--------------------------|
| Dining | +$932.20 | +$2,336.29 | +$0.00 | +$706.79 |
| Groceries | +$279.34 | +$1,150.18 | +$0.00 | +$256.01 |
| Retail | +$984.63 | +$1,824.30 | +$0.00 | +$599.63 |
| Subscriptions | +$10.99 | +$77.46 | +$0.00 | +$0.00 |
| Transit | +$677.61 | +$1,034.51 | +$0.00 | +$332.39 |
| Travel | +$1,005.93 | +$1,005.93 | +$0.00 | +$0.00 |
| Utilities | +$207.44 | +$266.84 | +$0.00 | +$0.00 |

So what: timing buys the trough, not the month end floor. The floor stays at -$80.24 under every aligned scenario because no forward target can escape the end of the binding 31 day month (2025-12), while the day 24 trough (2025-12-24) improves by up to $1,005.93. The reverse direction (Dining left late) can only game the month end sample point (+$706.79) without touching the true low, which is exactly why both metrics ship together. This is a counterfactual simulation, not a causal estimate: real merchants, rent, and autopay bills cannot in general be moved to an arbitrary day, so the deltas bound an opportunity rather than measure an achievable effect.

### F5: ranked interventions, the surge cap wins the floor

Question: Which intervention lifts the balance floor and tightens the drawdown the most?

Ranking rule: Primary key: minMonthEndDeltaCents descending. Secondary key: drawdownImprovementCents descending. rankByDrawdown gives the alternative ordering directly.

| Rank | Intervention | Floor delta | Drawdown improvement | Label |
|------|--------------|-------------|----------------------|-------|
| 1 | Cap the top surge category at its baseline rate | +$2,366.72 | -$306.64 | simulated counterfactual |
| 2 | Realign Dining to the income phase | +$0.00 | +$932.20 | simulated counterfactual |
| 3 | Consolidate subscriptions to a single cycle | +$0.00 | +$138.44 | simulated counterfactual |
| 4 | Fixed auto save on payday with emergency release | +$0.00 | +$0.00 | simulated counterfactual |

Headline: Cap the top surge category at its baseline rate lifts the minimum month end balance by $2,366.72 (floor moves from -$80.24 to +$2,286.48), and Dining realignment improves drawdown by $932.20. The auto save row is the honest null: on the combined view it moves neither metric, while deficit days stay at 86: the release fires on 28 days but only reverses the dips the saving itself creates (the held reserve funds exactly its own shortfall), so no genuine deficit day is covered and the reserve ends the year at $6,814.07. Combined view deltas are zero by construction; the full accounting (released days, lifted and created counts, reserve balance) is listed in auxiliary.

Every row in F5 is a simulated counterfactual: none of these rules was applied to the observed ledger, and each delta assumes behavior everywhere else stays fixed.

## Limitations

- Classification is per category, not per transaction: a category counts in full as structural or discretionary, so a category that mixes fixed bills with choices cannot be split internally.
- The lag window tests monthly recurrence only. Weekly locked behavior (for example weekday commuting in Transit) counts as discretionary here by construction; F2 measures that weekly pattern separately.
- One year of daily data gives 365 samples, so statistical power near the threshold is limited and a single billing drift can move a peak.
- Travel has fewer than the minimum debit rows for the lag test and is counted as discretionary because it cannot demonstrate recurrence.
- The mock ledger was generated with 28 to 32 day billing cycles, so this detector is validated against known construction rather than a real statement; the method itself does not use the generator.
- Shares are computed over a single year, so month shares mix season with one off events: the Travel month shares come from seven rows, below the reliability floor for any weekday reading.
- The holiday window (2025-11-15 to 2025-12-31, 47 days) is the mock generator's surge period; on real data the same rule should be rewritten against declared holiday dates before it is trusted.
- Verdict thresholds (1.25 holiday lift, 0.20 weekend deviation, 30 row floor) are declared design choices, not hypothesis tests; nearby values would relabel borderline categories such as Dining (weekend deviation 0.24) and Groceries (0.21).
- Day of week shares attribute spend to the posting date, not to the moment of consumption (a late night Saturday charge counts Saturday).
- Calendar driven versus behavioral is an interpretation of the pattern, not a causal test of why the person spent.
- Subscriptions and Utilities bill on fixed dates, so their calendar recurrence shows up in F1 while F2 correctly reports their seasonal shape as weak; recurrence and seasonality are different questions.
- One synthetic account and one year of daily rows (364 samples, 86 events): uncertainty around the test AUC is wide and no confidence interval is claimed.
- Strong concept drift: the account balance rises monotonically after the deficit season, so test months are out of distribution for the training months (train AUC versus test AUC in metrics shows the gap).
- The current closing balance is excluded by design, which keeps the model honest as a forward screen but caps discrimination; an autoregressive balance feature would score much higher and say less.
- Raw accuracy at the 0.5 threshold sits below the majority class baseline because the class balance shifts sharply between train and test; AUC is the headline metric and the confusion matrix is reported in full.
- Correlation, not causation: coefficients describe association inside this ledger. days_since_payday raising the odds does not mean waiting longer causes a deficit.
- Month indicators memorize this ledger's calendar (the November to April deficit season) and carry no meaning for another year.
- This is a counterfactual simulation, not a causal estimate: real merchants, rent, and autopay bills cannot in general be moved to an arbitrary day, so the deltas bound an opportunity rather than measure an achievable effect.
- Only timing moves: every scenario conserves the category total exactly, so an aligned scenario never improves the ledger by spending less.
- Month end floors are sample points. The forward shift can only move a debit forward to days 25 to 31 of the deposit month, and a spill past a 30 day month lands on day 1 to 3 of the next month, so no month end close can ever fall under the aligned rule; the binding floor here is December, a 31 day month that no forward target can escape, which is why the floor delta is exactly zero while the true intra month trough (troughDeltaCents) moves. Floors respond to amounts, which is F5's capping intervention, not to timing.
- The counterfactual holds behavior fixed: moving dining dates in the simulation does not change how much the person chooses to eat out, and the model assumes the person can and would move each payment.
- Paydays are one monthly deposit on the 25th in this ledger; biweekly or weekly pay cycles would need a different phase definition, though the recipe (phase to deposit window) transfers unchanged.
- Travel carries only seven debit rows, so its aligned delta is dominated by single splurges moving across the trough; read it as an upper bound on timing effects for rare large purchases rather than a habit effect.
- Daily closing balances are the sampling grid: intra day ordering between a moved debit and the deposit itself is not modeled beyond the row order rule.
- All four rows are simulated counterfactuals. Nothing here was observed; each delta is what the stated rule would produce under fixed behavior elsewhere in the ledger.
- Ranking is by minimum month end balance first and drawdown improvement second; a different priority reorders the list, which is why rankByDrawdown ships alongside rank.
- The surge cap changes amounts, not only timing: it models spending less during the holiday window, so its delta bounds a behavior change rather than a scheduling change.
- The consolidation row blends two effects (one cycle plus a post payday anchor) because a single cycle must sit somewhere; the anchor day is stated in the definition rather than hidden.
- The auto save row is an internal transfer: on the combined view of checking plus reserve the floor and the drawdown cannot move, which is a declared null, not an oversight. The deficit counts stay flat for an arithmetic reason: covering a genuine deficit day would require the held reserve to exceed itself plus the shortfall, because the holding that funds the release is what deepens the checking balance, so releases only reverse the dips the saving itself creates. The durable change is the reserve balance, reported in auxiliary.
- Intervention interactions are not modeled: applying every rule at once is not the sum of the rows, because each scenario starts from the observed ledger.
- All numbers come from the synthetic seed 20261107 ledger; deltas will differ on the real Tangerine dataset, though the simulation recipe is unchanged.
- Data: All numbers derive from data/mock/tangerine_transactions.csv, a SYNTHETIC mock ledger generated by scripts/generate_mock_ledger.py with seed 20261107. No real bank data appears anywhere in this repository. Every method here transfers unchanged to the real Tangerine dataset: only the input CSV path changes, because scripts/build_world.py already consumes either file.
- Correlation, not causation: every coefficient and every counterfactual delta is an association or a modeling result inside this ledger, never an identified causal effect.
- Reproducibility: pinned dependencies, logged seeds, time ordered split, and a verification step that recomputes the published numbers from source.
